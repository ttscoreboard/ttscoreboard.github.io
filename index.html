Privacy policy: Table Tennis Voice Scoring

This is the privacy policy of the Android app TT Scoreboard, published on Google Play as Table Tennis Voice Scoring by the developer TidyKit. It applies to version 1.0.0 of the app and later versions.

What the app collects

This app needs no account and works offline. Your matches, players, rule sets and settings stay on this phone, and the app itself collects nothing: it has no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting of its own, and it sends nothing to the developer. A result only leaves the phone when you share it yourself. Spoken announcements use the phone's text-to-speech engine and say only scores and calls, never names.

The microphone

Voice scoring uses the microphone only while voice scoring is on and the scoreboard is on screen, and while you run the mic check. Speech is recognised on the phone: the audio is processed in memory and then discarded, unless you record a voice session (below).

Voice session recordings

Recording voice sessions is a developer option for tuning voice scoring. It's off unless you turn on developer mode and agree before each recording, and it stops at the end of the match, when voice scoring is turned off, or an hour after you agreed. A recording holds the microphone's audio, which can include anyone talking nearby, a log of what voice scoring heard and did, the phone's make, model and Android version, the app's version, and the match's rules and the app's settings, but no player names. Recordings stay on this phone and leave it only if you share one yourself. Together they take at most 500 MB; past that, the oldest is deleted. You can delete them in Settings, Developer, or by uninstalling the app.

Google Play

Buying Premium goes through Google Play, which handles the payment; the app never sees your payment details. The app keeps only whether Premium is owned and how many trial matches have been used, on this phone. For the purchase the app includes Google Play's billing library, the app's only use of the internet. The billing library may send diagnostics to Google, such as the phone's make, model and Android build, its language and country, the mobile network, the time zone and the app's version. If you let your phone share usage and diagnostics with Google, Android may also send Google reports of the app's crashes, which Google shows the developer without saying who you are. Google handles this data under its own privacy policy, at policies.google.com/privacy.

Moving to a new phone

Android's phone-to-phone transfer can copy your matches, players, rule sets and settings to a new phone, including whether Premium is owned and the trial matches used. Nothing from the app is ever backed up to the cloud. Voice recordings and developer mode never move.

Keeping and deleting your data

Everything the app keeps is in its private storage on this phone, which other apps can't read, and stays there until you delete it. Delete matches in History, players in Players and rule sets in Settings, Rule sets. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, removes all of it, recordings included. The developer holds none of your data, except an email you send, which is used only to answer you and deleted once it's no longer needed. What the billing library sends is held by Google, under Google's policy.
